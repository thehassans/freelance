# The 2026 Guide to Freelance Taxes & Deductions

Tax season shouldn't be a surprise. For freelancers, every day is tax day. If you're not planning ahead, you're essentially giving yourself a 30% pay cut at the end of the year.

## 1. The 30% Rule

The simplest way to stay safe is to move 30% of **every single invoice payment** into a high-yield savings account. This isn't your money—it's the government's. By keeping it separate, you avoid the "Tax Cliff" in April.

## 2. Common Deductions

Don't leave money on the table. If it's "ordinary and necessary" for your business, it might be deductible:

- **Home Office**: A percentage of your rent/mortgage based on square footage.
- **Software Subscriptions**: From Adobe Creative Cloud to FreelancerKit.io.
- **Hardware**: That new M3 MacBook Pro isn't just a toy; it's a tool.
- **Education**: Courses, books, and conference tickets.

## 3. Quarterly Estimated Payments

In many jurisdictions, if you expect to owe more than $1,000, you must pay in quarters. Missing these can lead to "underpayment penalties."
