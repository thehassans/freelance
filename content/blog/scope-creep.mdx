# The Silent Killer: How to Stop Scope Creep Before It Starts

Scope creep is like a leak in your boat. At first, it's just a few drops—a "quick favor" here, a "small tweak" there. But before you know it, you're bailing out water just to stay afloat.

In this guide, we'll dive deep into:
1. Why scope creep happens
2. The red flags to watch for during onboarding
3. Tactical scripts to push back without losing the client

## Why it happens

Most freelancers fall into the trap of wanting to be "helpful." We want to exceed expectations. But without a clear boundary, "helpful" becomes "free labor."

> "If you don't set boundaries, your clients will set them for you—at your expense."

## The Red Flags

- "We'll figure out the details as we go."
- "This should be a quick fix."
- "Can you just add one more thing?"

## How to push back

When a client asks for extra work, don't just say no. Use the **"Yes, And"** method:

*"Yes, I can definitely add that feature! It's a great idea. Since it's outside our original scope, I'll send over a quick addendum with the adjusted timeline and fee for this addition."*
